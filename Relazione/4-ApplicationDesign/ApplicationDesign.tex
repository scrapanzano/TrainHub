The application design phase translated the results of the user research into a set of navigable interfaces. We first organized the required screens and their relationships, then created low-fidelity wireframes and a digital prototype in Figma. The prototype was used to establish the hierarchy of information, the recurring components, and the distinction between the member and professional experiences before implementation began.

\subsection{From Research to Design}\label{subsect:FromResearchToDesign}
% Breve: quali funzionalità il cap. 3 ha confermato/scartato, ponte diretto
% alle Personas/Scenarios/Affinity Diagram del cap. 3 (sul modello di BookIT
% 4.1 "Revisione delle funzionalità").

The three themes identified in the Affinity Diagram directly influenced the feature structure. The burden of digital fragmentation led to a single application containing workouts, nutrition, appointments, chat, and facility access. The communication difficulties reported by both user groups resulted in an explicit relationship between each member and their assigned professional, with a shared chat and plan-management tools. Finally, the demand for an all-in-one service confirmed that the member and professional areas had to belong to the same product rather than to two unrelated applications.

The scenarios helped establish the priority of each interaction. Workout logging had to remain quick enough to use between sets, while professional screens had to summarize multiple clients without forcing the trainer to open every individual record. Nutrition was kept intentionally simpler: professionals create and update plans, while members consult the assigned meals. Appointment booking, QR access, and push notifications complete the gym journey without replacing the central workout flow.
